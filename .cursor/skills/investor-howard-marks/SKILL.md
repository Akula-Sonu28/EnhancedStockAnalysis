# Howard Marks — Investment Framework

## Core philosophy

**Second-level thinking:** "It's good, therefore buy" is first-level. "It's good but better than price implies — wait, everyone loves it — then cautious" is second-level.

**Risk = probability of permanent loss**, not day-to-day volatility.

## How he chooses investments (especially credit/value)

### Cycle positioning
| Phase | Behavior | Marks action |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| Recovery | Fear easing | Buy quality distressed |
| Expansion | Optimism rising | Still find value |
| Exuberance | "This time different" | **Sell risk, raise cash** |
| Crisis | Forced selling | **Aggressive buying** |

### Risk checklist
- Where is market in **cycle of psychology**? (skepticism ↔ credulity)
- Is return **compensating for risk** taken?
- **Liquidity** — can you hold through drawdown?
- **Negative scenario** — survive?

### Stock/equity lens (via Oaktree equity evolution)
- **Quality of business** + **price** + **cycle**
- Avoid paying for perfection at peak cycle
- Prefer **survivors** with balance sheet in downturns

## Second-level thinking examples

| First-level | Second-level |
|-------------|--------------|
| Great company → buy | Great company but fully priced → wait |
| Bad news → sell | Bad news overdiscounted → buy |
| Low volatility = safe | Low vol often precedes complacency and hidden risk |

## Trends Marks emphasizes

- **Credit cycles** — defaults follow lending excess
- **Reflexivity of sentiment** — prices affect fundamentals (via access to capital)
- **Distressed debt** when cycles turn — Oaktree core
- **India:** NBFC/bank stress cycles, real estate downcycles — contrarian entry when others can't lend

## How he makes money

- **Buy when others must sell** (forced sellers)
- **Avoid losers** in exuberance — preservation of capital
- **Asymmetric payoffs** — limited downside, substantial upside
- **Patience** — wait for pitch in your strike zone

## What Marks avoids

- **Market timing** as precision science — but **aggressiveness varies** with prices
- Strategies requiring **being right on edge every time**
- Leverage that converts volatility into permanence
- Herding into fashionable assets
