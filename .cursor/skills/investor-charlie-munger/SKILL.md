# Charlie Munger — Investment Framework

## Core philosophy

**Worldly wisdom** across disciplines + **invert always invert**. Make fewer, bigger, higher-quality decisions. Money comes from not being stupid, buying great businesses, and waiting.

## How he chooses stocks (with Buffett at Berkshire)

1. **Filter stupidity first** — what kills this business? (inversion)
2. **Quality bar higher than Graham** — "good business at fair price" beats "fair business at good price"
3. **Mental models** — psychology, incentives, competitive destruction, scale economics
4. **Concentration** — few bets where edge is clearest
5. **Patience + no activity for its own sake**

## Mental models for stock selection

| Model | Application |
|-------|-------------|
| **Incentives** | How are managers paid? Promoter aligned? |
| **Social proof** | Is crowd wrong at extremes? (contrarian input) |
| **Deprival superreaction** | Loss aversion drives market panics — opportunity |
| **Scale economies** | Winner-take-most in distribution (Costco, BNSF) |
| **Opportunity cost** | Compare to next best idea, not cash idle |
| **Circle of competence** | Narrow but deep |
| **Lollapalooza** | Multiple factors combine (brand + scale + regulation) |

## Selection criteria

- **Durable competitive advantage** (same as Buffett but stricter on complexity)
- **Management integrity** — non-negotiable
- **Rational capital deployment**
- **Understand second-order effects** (e.g. cheap credit → future NPA)
- **Avoid:** heavy capex treadmills, tech obsolescence risk unless deeply understood

## Trends / themes Munger favored

- **Costco-style** — extreme efficiency + customer loyalty
- **Chinese consumer** (BYD — controversial, shows openness when understood)
- **Banks/financials** when risk culture sane
- **Avoid crypto, much of fintech hype** — "rat poison squared" on Bitcoin

## How he makes money

- Partner in Berkshire compounding machine
- **Sit-on-your-ass** investing — low turnover, tax efficient
- **Learning machines** — continuous reading → better filters → fewer errors
- **Checklist discipline** before any commitment

## Munger "avoid" list

- Leverage (personal or corporate excessive)
- Envy and jealousy driving trades
- Over-diversification ("diworsification")
- Investment banking narratives without substance
- Hubris after success
