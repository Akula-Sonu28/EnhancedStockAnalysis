# Joel Greenblatt — Investment Framework

## Core philosophy

**"Buying good companies at cheap prices"** — two metrics rank the universe. For advanced: **special situations** where corporate events create mispricing.

## Magic Formula (systematic)

Rank all stocks on:

1. **Return on Capital (ROIC)** — high = good business
2. **Earnings Yield** (EBIT / Enterprise Value) — high = cheap

**Combined rank** → buy top 20–30, hold ~1 year, rebalance

### Why it works (historically)
- Market overreacts to **short-term noise** on quality
- **Mean reversion** in multiples
- **Discipline** of systematic rules beats emotion

### Caveats
- Underperforms in **growth/momentum bubbles** (can lag 2–3 years)
- Needs **patience** and faith in process
- Adjust for **leverage, cyclical earnings** — use normalized EBIT

## Special situations ( enterprising )

| Type | Idea |
|------|------|
| **Spinoffs** | Parent dumps "lesser" unit → forced selling creates cheapness |
| **Merger arbitrage** | Spread on announced deals |
| **Restructuring** | Complexity scares away buyers |
| **Rights offerings / distressed** | Need specialized work |

## How he makes money

- **Quant value spread** — many names, statistical edge
- **Event-driven** — catalyst closes gap to fair value
- **Education edge** — small investors avoid institutions' constraints
- **Compounding** through repeated cycles of rank → hold → refresh

## What Greenblatt avoids

- Paying for **hype without earnings support**
- Abandoning formula during underperformance
- Ignoring **debt** in ROIC/EV calculations
- Illiquid names for large capital

## NSE Magic Formula adaptation

```text
Universe: liquid Nifty 500 or custom CSV
ROIC: EBIT / (Net working capital + Net fixed assets)
Earnings yield: EBIT / EV
Exclude: banks/NBFCs (different capital structure), extreme cyclicals at peak EBIT
Rebalance: annually or semi-annually; watch tax/STT
```
