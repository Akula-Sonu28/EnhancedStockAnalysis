# Philip Fisher — Investment Framework

## Core philosophy

Buy a **small number of outstanding growth companies** and hold **years to decades**. Primary research is **scuttlebutt** — talking to customers, competitors, ex-employees, suppliers — not just spreadsheets.

## Fifteen points (condensed checklist)

1. Products/services with **sufficient market potential** for years of sales growth
2. Management determination to develop new products/processes as market matures
3. **R&D** effectiveness relative to size
4. **Sales organization** above average
5. **Profit margins** worth protecting; evidence they will maintain/improve
6. Maintaining/improving margins indicates **cost controls**
7. **Labor and personnel relations** outstanding
8. **Executive depth** — succession works
9. **Management depth** throughout company
10. **Cost analysis and accounting controls** excellent
11. Industry-specific **competitive advantages** (Fisher details per industry)
12. **Long-range profit outlook** — planning beyond next quarter
13. **Equity financing** only when dilution justified by growth returns
14. **Management candor** with investors when things go wrong
15. **Integrity** of management unquestioned

## How he chooses stocks

| Phase | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Scuttlebutt** | 360° view from outsiders before meeting management |
| **Management meeting** | Verify integrity; avoid promotional CEOs |
| **Financial confirm** | Margins, growth consistency, R&D/sales ratios |
| **Buy** | Small position; add on confirmation over years |
| **Hold** | Through volatility if thesis intact |
| **Sell** | Management deterioration, competitive edge lost, opportunity cost extreme |

## Trends Fisher rode (historical)

- **Technology & advanced manufacturing** (Motorola, Texas Instruments era)
- **Companies investing in future products** while profitable today
- **Consumer brands** with distribution excellence

## How he makes money

- **Compounding sales and margins** over long periods
- **Low turnover** — minimizes taxes and mistakes
- **Concentration** in best-scuttlebutt names
- **Early identification** of excellence before broad recognition

## What Fisher avoids

- Management without integrity (absolute pass)
- Short-term earnings management
- Businesses without reinvestment opportunity
- Overpaying even for quality (but pays more than Graham)

## NSE scuttlebutt sources

- Channel partners, distributors, ex-employees (LinkedIn), customer reviews
- Industry association data, import/export stats
- Competitor conference calls mentioning the company
- Promoter track record on other group companies
